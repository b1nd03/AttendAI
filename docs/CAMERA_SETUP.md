# Camera setup and attendance checks

Start with a check-in lane or doorway where people face the camera and light falls evenly on their faces. Use a wider preview for room coverage. Confirm the source resolution and usable face detail with an on-site test.

## Check the actual feed

1. Add the individual IP/RTSP channel in **Live Monitor → Connect a camera**. Use the channel’s main stream for a check-in candidate. Choose **Setup test** to measure a view or **Preview only** for coverage. The new camera dialog defaults to **Setup test**. For an existing camera, change **Manage camera → Operating mode** and click **Apply mode**.
2. Keep the test open and walk through the intended check-in point at normal speed. Check incoming and analysis resolutions, frames with a detected face, frames with a usable face and the largest measured face. A usable frame passes the workspace size, lighting and sharpness checks. Empty frames count in the totals. Counters restart when the feed reconnects, resolution or quality settings change, or the operating mode changes.
3. Check at the actual decision point. Aim for several usable frames on each walk. A large maximum from a single close frame can hide failures at the intended line. If the incoming frame is larger than the analyzed frame, try **Room-distance detection** with the main stream. If the face is small in the main stream, use optical zoom, a narrower field of view, or a closer check-in point. Keep the full route visible in the overview feed.
4. Test both directions, normal speed, two people together and the usual lighting. Once capture is consistent, run a supervised matching trial with authorized enrolled and unenrolled participants. Compare each expected person, camera, direction and time with the log. Tally missed arrivals, wrong names, false check-ins for unenrolled visitors and duplicate records separately. Only leave live matching enabled on a view that passes your trial. Use Preview only for overview feeds.

Setup test pauses matching, attendance, face saving and automatic learning for the selected camera. Preview only also stops detection on that feed. The mode is saved across reconnects and restarts. The setup counters are temporary. Both modes leave the other cameras running under their individual settings.

Use [shadow replay](FACEMOE_PILOT.md) on original recordings to separate quality, identity, liveness and crossing failures before a live trial. Include supervised photo and screen-replay cases in the liveness trial.

## Research behind the checks

- **Distance and detail.** NIST found face size and detector confidence useful when predicting recognition performance in video, and evaluated roll, yaw and environmental factors. Axis describes how optics, compression and light affect usable detail even at the same resolution. The IEC thresholds in the Axis guide apply to human review. AttendAI uses its own face-size setting, which defaults to 90 pixels in the shorter dimension of the face box in the analyzed frame. [NIST video quality study](https://www.nist.gov/publications/identifying-face-quality-and-factor-measures-video) · [Axis pixel density guide](https://whitepapers.axis.com/en-us/pixel-density-based-on-iec-62676-4-2025)
- **Frame the check-in lane.** For a frontal face about 0.16 m wide, a planning target of 120 pixels corresponds to a scene width of about 5.1 m at the check-in distance in a 3840-pixel stream, or 2.6 m in a 1920-pixel stream. The estimate uses `scene width = horizontal stream pixels × face width ÷ target face pixels`. For a 4K view, 5.1 m across is roughly a 29° horizontal field of view at 10 m or 19° at 15 m. These are optical planning estimates. Measure the actual stream at the marked point. [Axis pixel density guide](https://whitepapers.axis.com/en-us/pixel-density-based-on-iec-62676-4-2025)
- **Glass doors and wet areas.** Check the face exposure while people move. WDR can reveal faces against bright doorways, and its motion artifacts can vary by camera and exposure. Compare motion, shadows and glare under the site's actual lighting. [Axis WDR white paper](https://whitepapers.axis.com/en-us/wide-dynamic-range)
- **Verify unknowns and knowns separately.** NIST's open-set video evaluation treats matching enrolled people and ignoring unenrolled people as separate outcomes. Capture quality measures whether there is enough detail to attempt a match. A walkthrough with actual participants and the intended gallery measures false matches and misses. [NIST Face in Video Evaluation](https://pages.nist.gov/frvt/html/frte_five.html) · [NIST Face Image Quality evaluation](https://pages.nist.gov/frvt/html/frvt_quality.html)
