# Face recognition at 5–15 metres

This field-test plan compares models for near and distant entrances. The field-of-view values below are geometric estimates.

The [GPU pilot guide](FACEMOE_PILOT.md) explains how to compare FaceMoE and the existing pipeline on recorded approaches.

## Model choice

**Keep SFace and AuraFace R100 as the operational baseline. Pilot FaceMoE Swin-B WebFace4M, fine-tuned on BRIAR, as the first accuracy upgrade for the distant entrance.** Run the challenger on a few good crops from each eligible track while the existing model continues to handle the near views. Begin with shadow results that do not write attendance. CVLFace ViT-B KP-RPE is the comparison candidate if the goal is a single challenger for mixed near and distant views. The FaceMoE paper also reports **76.42%** TinyFace rank-1 and **74.77%** BRIAR rank-1 for WebFace12M models fine-tuned on their respective datasets. The published model card points to WebFace4M weights, so those higher figures should be treated as paper results. TinyFace averages 20×16 pixels per face and is useful as a stress test. Rank-1 measures whether the correct enrolled identity ranks first. For attendance, set the decision threshold using open-set identification on unknown visitors, then compare misses at that threshold. NIST FIVE is a closer video test design because it includes multi-person video, compression, low resolution and different camera angles. ## Camera geometry

For a frontal face near the image centre, estimate face width in pixels as `image width in pixels × 0.16 m ÷ (2 × distance in metres × tan(horizontal field of view ÷ 2))`. Use actual horizontal field of view at the selected optical zoom and actual source-stream width. The 16 cm face width is an illustrative assumption. Axis’s 2025 IEC guide concerns human interpretation and gives separate consideration to software analytics. AttendAI’s current setting uses **90 source pixels across the smaller face-box dimension** before matching. Use roughly **120 pixels across the face** as an initial installation target to leave margin and verify the detected box on actual clips. [Axis pixel density guidance](https://whitepapers.axis.com/en-us/pixel-density-based-on-iec-62676-4-2025) · [AttendAI quality gate](../backend/recognition.py)

| Horizontal field of view at 3840 px | 5 m | 10 m | 15 m |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 90° | 61 px | 31 px | 20 px |
| 60° | 106 px | 53 px | 35 px |
| 35° | 195 px | 97 px | 65 px |
| 25° | 277 px | 139 px | 92 px |
| 20° | 348 px | 174 px | 116 px |

At 15 m, about **19° horizontal field of view** gives 120 pixels across a 16 cm face near the centre of a 3840-pixel stream. That corresponds to a scene width of about **5.1 m** at the capture line. Use an optical zoom or a closer entrance view, and measure real faces across the walking path to account for angle, blur, backlighting, glare and compression. At 1920 pixels the table values halve. Digital zoom of the same frame changes the display size while preserving its source detail. AttendAI’s normal analysis path downsizes a 4K frame to a 1280-pixel long edge. On a server-connected entrance camera, enable **Room-distance detection** to retain native detail. [AttendAI frame scheduler](../backend/monitoring.py)

## Inference and attendance design

1. Reserve the main stream and expensive face inference for the entrance or another defined capture line. Keep wide views for coverage. Use native-resolution face crops and collect the sharpest, distinct, reasonably frontal frames from one track. Compare quality-weighted aggregation with the existing vote rule in shadow mode. Body detections can locate head regions. Only matching faces with sufficient evidence contribute to attendance. [CAFace video feature aggregation](https://cvlab.cse.msu.edu/project-caface.html)
2. The local host has an RTX 3050 Ti Laptop GPU with 4 GB of VRAM, and AttendAI currently runs CPU inference. The previous six-feed CPU smoke test reached a median of 1.8 analyzed fps per source on 512×512 single-face fixtures. With five votes and regular sampling, even that example takes about 2.2 seconds from the first to the fifth usable observation. Use one warm challenger instance, a bounded best-crop queue and an isolated CUDA pilot. Verify that an accelerated export agrees with reference outputs before measuring latency, peak VRAM and dropped work under six streams. [Local verification](VERIFICATION.md) · [ONNX Runtime CUDA execution provider](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/CUDA-ExecutionProvider.html)
3. Maintain a separate gallery and thresholds for each model version. Build it from supervised enrollment images and compare models at the same false positive identification rate per independent unknown approach. Require calibrated identity evidence and a separate liveness decision. For the liveness test, use authorized live approaches, printed photos and screen presentations under the actual camera settings. [NIST 1:N identification](https://pages.nist.gov/frvt/html/frvt1N.html) · [NIST presentation attack detection](https://www.nist.gov/publications/face-analysis-technology-evaluation-fate-part-10-performance-passive-software-based)
4. Use unaltered source crops as identity evidence in the initial pilot. The October 2026 CVBL-CCTV-Face preprint reports that one tuned restoration model improved top-1 identification from 62.60% to 70.08% on its benchmark. Some other settings reduced accuracy, and the paper leaves false accepts and latency for future work. An August 2026 study also found that the best synthetic degradation setting did not transfer directly to native low-resolution imagery. Test any restoration separately at a fixed false-match rate before using it in an attendance decision. [CVBL-CCTV-Face paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.36781v1#S6) · [Synthetic-to-native study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06580)

## Field acceptance test

Record authorized approach clips at 5, 10 and 15 m with known participants and people outside the gallery. Use independent enrollment, calibration and held-out sessions. Include walking pace, groups, hats or goggles, side views, glare and different lighting. Test with all six feeds running. Log source face size, usable frames per pass, wrong names, missed check-ins, liveness failures, time to decision and duplicate events. Compare the current SFace plus AuraFace pipeline against FaceMoE using the same held-out approaches. Choose the stronger challenger only if it reduces distant misses at the same false-positive rate, maintains near-view performance and finishes before people leave the capture area. Set the rollout target before seeing the held-out results. Zero wrong matches in a small trial still leaves substantial statistical uncertainty. [NIST video evaluation design](https://pages.nist.gov/frvt/html/frte_five.html)
